# Frameworks and implementation

Use this reference when a framework will improve a decision, explain a causal mechanism or make implementation inspectable. Framework names do not earn space by themselves. Select tools after defining the argument.

## 1. Select the framework's job

| Question | Useful tool | Expected result | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Which barrier must change? | Issue tree with source-backed leaves | A causal diagnosis linked to design responses. | Decorative branches repeating the same symptom. |
| What work does each participant need done? | Jobs/value mapping or value proposition canvas | A concrete participation/incentive argument. | Invented persona biography or unsupported motivation. |
| Which external conditions shape execution? | PESTEL | Prioritized implications for operation. | Six generic paragraphs about the country. |
| Which capabilities need attention? | IFE | Explicit internal strengths/weaknesses and priorities. | Rating unbuilt capabilities as proven advantages. |
| How well does the arrangement respond externally? | EFE | Assessed responses to opportunities/threats. | Treating opportunity size as response quality. |
| Which actions follow the diagnosis? | SWOT/TOWS | Specific SO/ST/WO/WT decisions, owners and stages. | Repeating every PESTEL/IFE/EFE interpretation. |
| Why choose this arrangement? | Competitor/alternative comparison | Shared criteria and contextual tradeoffs. | Straw alternatives or unsupported self-scores. |
| What shapes negotiating/adoption pressures? | Five Forces | Consequences for pricing, partnerships and positioning. | A five-label checklist with no decision. |
| Is an advantage defensible? | VRIO | Separate value, rarity, imitation and organization judgments. | Assuming an interface is rare because it looks distinctive. |
| Who can authorize and perform work? | RACI or decision-rights table | One accountable owner and clear execution/consultation. | Calling everyone responsible without authority. |
| What must happen before service begins? | Gantt and dependency table | Dated capability/resource sequence. | Bars that contradict revenue or staffing start dates. |
| What happens when service fails? | Risk register and exception flow | Triggers, response, owner and resources. | A heatmap with no operating response. |
| How much can be reached or purchased? | TAM/SAM/SOM | Traceable market/coverage boundaries. | Calling an unfiltered population actual demand. |
| What contribution follows from activity? | Theory of change and SMART measures | Defined outputs/outcomes and reporting. | Labelling expenditures or interface events as final impact. |
| Which uncertain inputs change a decision? | Sensitivity/Monte Carlo | A useful range, key drivers and capital/operating implications. | Elaborate simulation with invented empirical probabilities. |

A short essay may need only an issue tree, operating flow, timeline and a few financial/impact tables. A detailed business dossier may justify more. Retain requested frameworks while consolidating repeated explanations. The body still needs to explain the resulting decision.

## 2. Issue tree and response mapping

Start with a clear objective or consequential problem. Divide it into multiple distinct subproblems where the real process requires them. Continue to specific root causes that the proposed mechanism can address. Prefer a horizontal reading order when the author's reference style and page size suit it:

**Goal/problem → subproblem/dimension → specific issue → root cause.**

Three columns may suffice for a simpler diagnosis. Do not add a dimension merely to imitate a reference image. Copy layout lessons, never labels from an unrelated example. A source image can contain template mistakes; inspect whether its stated objective, branches and leaves actually belong together.

Test each leaf:

1. Is it a cause, constraint, symptom, design inference or outcome? Name it accurately.
2. Is the statement distinct from nearby leaves?
3. What source, quantitative datum or transparent reasoning supports it?
4. What population, period and denominator apply?
5. Which mechanism responds to it, through which actor and action?
6. What remains outside the proposed response?

Do not invent a percentage so every branch appears numerically symmetrical. Some leaves may be supported by qualitative policy diagnosis, documented operating constraints or a reasoned inference. Preserve that evidence type. A statistical association is not automatically a causal effect.

Use a companion native table for evidence detail if the tree becomes unreadable. A useful mapping is:

| Leaf ID | Root cause | Evidence and scope | Design response | Observable change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1, etc. | One precise cause. | Citation, locator, unit/sample and material boundary. | Named actor/action. | The relevant output or decision. |

Keep enough evidence beside the diagram's leaves for their meaning to remain clear; put long locators/scopes in the companion table. The main analysis should lead with the tree's insight and then substantiate it, rather than announce that a framework has been used.

Check the response chain in both directions: every central mechanism addresses a diagnosed barrier, and every barrier promised to be solved has an actual response. A feature that does neither may be outside scope or an unnecessary distraction.

## 3. Strategic matrices and weighted scores

Treat weights and ratings as explicit team judgments unless they have an actual empirical elicitation method. State what the scale means. Do not invent expert approval, respondent counts or statistical confidence for planning scores.

For a weighted matrix with nonnegative weights wᵢ and ratings rᵢ:

**Σwᵢ = 1; scoreᵢ = wᵢ × rᵢ; total = Σ(wᵢ × rᵢ).**

Check sums and products from unrounded values. Use a small legend inside or directly below the exhibit. Interpret the resulting priority rather than explaining the framework's textbook history. A precise-looking total does not remove the judgment embedded in the inputs.

### PESTEL

Choose factors that affect this proposal's operation. If weighted, define whether a high rating means high priority, favorable conditions, exposure or response quality. Do not switch meanings between rows. End with the concrete allocation or design priority.

### IFE and EFE

Keep the two meanings separate. IFE ratings assess internal strengths/weaknesses under a declared scale. EFE ratings assess the proposed response to external opportunities/threats. Large opportunities do not automatically deserve high response ratings.

Preserve the factors, weights and ratings needed to inspect the result. Put resulting action priorities in TOWS or the implementation plan instead of reproducing a full action column in every matrix. If scores concern present commissioning readiness, identify that scope once; do not describe the complete proposed system as an achieved operating capability.

### SWOT and TOWS

Use a compact SWOT summary only if it helps the reader see the factors carried into TOWS. Write TOWS as decisions:

- SO: how strengths exploit a relevant opportunity.
- ST: how strengths address a real threat.
- WO: what capability or arrangement closes a weakness to use an opportunity.
- WT: what commitment is limited or redesigned to reduce combined exposure.

Add an owner, result and stage. Remove repeated tutorials and generic “improve collaboration” actions. An action should be specific enough to affect scope, resources, authority or sequence.

### Comparison, Five Forces and VRIO

Compare actual alternatives available to the target group. Include existing collection channels, manual coordination, dedicated services or doing nothing when relevant. A competitor with a narrower scope may still be a valuable partner or better choice for a particular workload.

Avoid a self-awarded weighted competitor score unless criteria, rating evidence and scale are clear. A table of observed capabilities and contextual tradeoffs is often more credible. Mark unknowns as unknown; do not assign a low rating to a competitor simply because its public page does not disclose a feature.

For VRIO, assess V, R, I and O separately. A capability can be valuable but common, difficult to imitate but not yet organized, or an execution need rather than a defensible resource. Treat relationships, repeatable records and operating competence according to their actual stage.

## 4. Participants, operations and accountability

Define the work and resource bargain. For each actor, specify:

- What they receive or need accomplished.
- What work, data, capacity, money or permission they contribute.
- Who can accept the work and change its terms.
- What makes participation rational under the stated conditions.
- What happens when they decline, cancel or cannot fulfill the commitment.

Make the proposal visible as one operating system. Follow a representative unit from request through planning, authorization, execution, acceptance and the next required outcome. Separate a preview from approval, a plan from execution, a pickup from receipt and a receipt from final recovery/use where those stages differ.

Use an explicit exception path. Preserve the real current holder of an item or responsibility; a cancellation must not fictitiously undo an event that already occurred. Identify who owns an open case, what decision is needed, who pays for additional work and what evidence closes it.

A RACI table should have exactly one accountable A per activity, including A/R combinations. R performs, A has final accountability, C is consulted and I is informed. Check that the actor with A also has the authority/resources described in the operating plan. Multiple responsible performers may be appropriate. Do not force RACI onto a simple task if a short decision-rights table is clearer.

Separate specialist review from the original decision when a dispute or competence requirement makes independence important. Do not imply a software flag supplies professional authorization, identity verification, physical inspection or real data erasure.

## 5. Timeline and expansion

Build the timeline from dependencies rather than evenly spreading bars. Align at least these schedules where relevant:

| Capability | Dependencies to reconcile |
|---|---|
| Paid service begins | Contract, payer, trained staff, accepted operator/destination and functioning records. |
| New customer/program added | Sales/authorization, onboarding, working capital, fulfillment and supervision capacity. |
| Billing/cash collected | Actual service unit, recognition point, payment terms and reconciliation. |
| New region opened | Local permissions/access, operators, receiving capability and funded obligations. |
| Data-sensitive work begins | Appropriate methods, competent personnel, authorization and evidence. |
| Strategic scale achieved | Repeatable operating arrangements and resources, not a date alone. |

Use a detailed commissioning/year-one Gantt and a longer capability roadmap when the proposal calls for both. The author's ten-year horizon in JejakBalik was a project decision, not a universal duration. Do not extrapolate precise ten-year revenues merely because the strategic diagram spans ten years.

State milestone outputs and the decision to proceed. A pilot is one complete first configuration within a route to sustained operation. Explain what is repeated, what adapts locally and who brings the resources into the next area. Do not end a national-scale proposal at “conduct a pilot.”

Reconcile dates between the prose, Gantt, staffing, financial model, marketing plan and contribution projection. If first revenue occurs before the system's necessary capability exists, fix the contradiction rather than hiding it behind a broad timeline bar.

## 6. Risk and data handling

For each material risk, record a trigger, consequence, initial severity/likelihood, response, owner, required resource and residual target where useful. Define the rating scale. A score is a prioritization device, not a measured probability unless evidence actually establishes it.

Use the heatmap for the positions it actually depicts. If it shows initial risks only, its caption must not claim that it also plots residual/control targets. Keep risk IDs consistent with the register. The mitigation must appear in operations or budget when it requires staff, equipment, reserves or specialist work.

Data and regulated-category checks should match the proposal. Identify authorization, access, retention, instruction changes, operator competence, handling eligibility and evidence of actual work. A prototype's local state does not establish a shared authenticated record. A status marked “reviewed” does not establish media sanitization or downstream processing.

Use current authoritative sources for legal requirements. Do not assume every local courier, facility or institution can handle every material category. Present an eligible operating route with clear boundaries, not an unsolicited compliance lecture or a claim of certification.

## 7. Contribution and measurement

Build the theory of change through distinct stages:

**Inputs → activities → outputs → service outcomes → physical/social outcomes → longer-term contribution.**

Specify which stage a number measures. Planning coverage, receipt counts, accepted mass, completed treatment, extended useful life and net environmental impact are different quantities. State what evidence would support each. Do not turn spending or paid capacity into extra profit, jobs or avoided emissions without a valid method.

For a SMART measure, define the numerator, denominator, period, target, owner, evidence and action when performance departs from the target. Avoid percentages whose reporting population changes silently. A complaint-response rate might use cases whose response deadline falls within the period rather than all complaints ever received; choose and state a defensible denominator.

Use contribution language for SDGs and policy goals. Link the exact target to the relevant physical/service pathway. A waste-reduction target is supported by repair/reuse/processing outcomes, not merely by expenditure on staff. Targets and program activities do not establish the eventual global indicator change.

Keep headline intended contribution in the essay, including a range where assumptions justify it. The appendix carries detailed definitions, scenario calculations, sensitivity and account boundaries.
