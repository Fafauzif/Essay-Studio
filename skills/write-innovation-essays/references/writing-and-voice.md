# Writing and voice

Use this reference for drafting, substantive editing, humanization and compression in Indonesian or English. Preserve the author's meaning, facts, numbers, quotations and citations. These preferences generalize from the author's supplied work and feedback; the current competition and explicit author instruction still control.

## 1. The intended reading experience

Write an explanation that makes the reader understand and judge a developed proposal. Let specific actors, mechanisms, relationships and consequential numbers carry the ambition. Avoid generic claims of transformation, decorative novelty labels and ceremonial declarations that a solution is “holistic,” “strategic” or “revolutionary.”

The author favors connected prose and meaningful transitions. A paragraph may be relatively long when its reasoning earns the space. Avoid chopping a sustained explanation into disconnected one-sentence assertions, making every paragraph the same length or ending each with a conspicuous final verdict. Technical precision belongs in the essay when it helps the reader assess the case; the raw inspection detail can sit behind it.

Natural language is not a detector-evasion trick. Do not add fake field experiences, invented interviews, deliberate mistakes, exaggerated personal emotion or unsupported anecdotes to imitate human authorship. Learn the user's decisions and cadence from real samples. If a phrase is unnatural, change its information order and specificity, not merely its synonyms.

## 2. Paragraph and sentence decisions

Give each paragraph one principal job. Use the sequence that serves that job:

| Job | Useful sequence | Reader should leave knowing |
|---|---|---|
| Context | Relevant condition → selective evidence → present consequence | Why this problem matters here. |
| Diagnosis | Barrier → scoped evidence → causal interpretation | What the proposal must change. |
| Mechanism | Actor/action → change in process or incentives → intended effect | How the arrangement produces its advantage. |
| Comparison | Credible alternative → shared criteria → contextual tradeoff | Why the chosen arrangement is justified. |
| Projection | Relevant assumption boundary → result → practical meaning | What the result supports and what determines its headroom. |
| Implementation | Capability established → owner/resources → next stage | How the proposal becomes sustained operation. |
| Synthesis | Established barrier/mechanism → bounded judgment | What should be pursued and why. |

Start from something the reader recognizes, then introduce the new relationship. Explain a function before its acronym or branded component. Replace an invented label when ordinary language is clearer. Define necessary terms once and use them consistently.

Choose active verbs and responsible actors. Replace “a coordination mechanism will be conducted” with who coordinates which work. Avoid stacking actor-role semicolons into a compressed specification list when the paragraph's job is explanation. Semicolons remain legitimate punctuation in complex sentences, definitions or formulas; the author's criticism was of mechanical cadence, not an automatic ban on a character.

Read a continuous page. Several long sentences may need a simpler interpretation between them. Several short sentences may need connection. Neither repair requires a fixed sentence-length rule.

## 3. Opening and concept introduction

Use a concrete, evidence-led background. Narrow from significance and relevant scale to the specific unresolved barrier. Explain existing responses before asserting the gap. Do not begin by defining an ordinary dictionary term, reciting broad technological eras or listing national statistics without showing their relation to the intervention.

An inverted-triangle opening is a useful preference, not permission to pad several levels of irrelevant context. The original JejakBalik correction asked for 3–6 introduction citations; another project's evidence count must fit its claims, guidebook and space. Do not turn that count into a universal quota.

When the agreed architecture reserves the proposed answer for Gagasan or an equivalent section, keep substantive solution exposition out of the background/analysis. A project title can still name the proposal. At the introduction point, give a plain definition that identifies the actual arrangement, participants, operator/resource model and work performed. A judge should understand the proposal with appendices closed.

An illustrative transaction can then make the operating mechanism concrete. Label invented scenario inputs as an illustration where their numbers affect interpretation. Do not disguise a constructed example as an author-observed experience.

## 4. Evidence, projections and confidence

Use confident verbs for adopted design choices: the proposed arrangement assigns, connects, funds, schedules, reconciles or routes work. Support the explanation through evidence and causal reasoning. Avoid turning a proposal into a catalogue of everything not yet measured.

Match factual grammar to actual status:

| Meaning | Appropriate wording pattern | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed arrangement | “The coordinator assigns…” / “Koordinator menetapkan…” in an established proposal context. | Implying an actual signed contract through a named role. |
| Planning projection | “Under the team assumptions…” / “Pada estimasi tim…” beside the relevant result. | “The service achieved…” for an unexecuted scenario. |
| Target | “The first-year target is…” / “Sasaran tahun pertama…” | Reporting a target as a measured output. |
| Reported research | “The survey found…” with population/year and accurate strength. | Turning correlation or one locality into general causal proof. |
| Actual demonstration | State what the documented test or capture did. | Treating local interface status as authenticated field operation. |

Place a decisive limit near the claim it changes. Repeat a brief boundary when an exhibit is detached, a denominator changes or a new conclusion would otherwise mislead. Remove repeated “needs validation” endings that add no new decision. A real uncertainty deserves an evidence source, assumption/sensitivity, design response or targeted commissioning decision.

Do not use “model” as a verbal apology in every paragraph. Use it where the mathematical method is the subject. A sentence about an intended service can describe that service directly while its associated table correctly labels projections.

## 5. Endings and transitions

Use a paragraph ending for the implication of what was explained or the next question it creates. Place the appendix reference beside the claim, then complete the reasoning if needed. Avoid serial endings that advertise the appendix, declare a key takeaway or demand further validation.

Choose transitions that express the relationship:

- Cause: explain the actual reason rather than automatically inserting “therefore.”
- Continuation: develop the consequence of the previous paragraph's last point.
- Contrast: name the relevant tradeoff when it matters; do not manufacture an opponent for rhetorical effect.
- Scale: explain what changes when the arrangement moves to another setting.
- Finance: connect work/resource obligations to the person or institution paying.

Read consecutive first and last sentences. They should build the same proposition with new information. If several paragraphs establish the same urgency or repeat the full three-part thesis, merge or repurpose them.

The conclusion synthesizes the case established in the essay. Preserve the decisive condition when it changes the recommendation. Do not introduce a new number, source, operating rule or social benefit there. Avoid a final sentence that simply lists every component again if the essay has already made their relationship clear.

## 6. Constructed bilingual examples

The examples below are teaching examples authored for this kit. They are not extracts from reference essays, verified empirical findings or claims about an operating project. Add real evidence when using a factual premise in an actual submission.

### A. Replace generic urgency with the relevant question

Weak Indonesian:

> Di era digital yang berkembang pesat, inovasi merupakan langkah strategis yang sangat penting bagi masa depan berkelanjutan.

Stronger construction:

> Jadwal layanan yang tidak sesuai kebutuhan peserta membuat akses sulit dipenuhi meskipun fasilitas telah tersedia. Persoalannya terletak pada cara kapasitas tersebut ditawarkan dan dikoordinasikan.

English equivalent:

> A facility can exist while its service remains difficult to access. The proposal addresses how available capacity is offered, scheduled and coordinated around participants' needs.

Why it works: it identifies a relationship the later mechanism can address. In a real essay, support the observed access premise with an appropriate source rather than treating this sentence as evidence.

### B. Explain the arrangement before naming components

Weak Indonesian:

> Program ini terdiri atas SmartHub, EcoConnect, DataCare, dan ImpactPlus yang saling terintegrasi.

Stronger construction:

> Institusi membeli satu program untuk pesertanya. Koordinator menyusun jadwal, memastikan pelaksana menerima pekerjaan, dan menggabungkan catatan pemenuhan sehingga pembeli dapat meninjau biaya serta kewajiban yang masih terbuka.

English equivalent:

> The institution buys a program for its participants. A coordinator arranges the schedule, confirms that operators accept the work, and reconciles fulfillment records so the buyer can review expenditure and open obligations.

Why it works: actors, work and purchase value are visible before brand labels. It specifies a proposal; it does not claim that a named institution has purchased it.

### C. Give finance an operating meaning

Weak Indonesian:

> Hasil perhitungan menunjukkan pendapatan, biaya, NPV, dan payback period yang dapat dilihat di Lampiran X. Namun, masih diperlukan validasi.

Stronger construction:

> Kontribusi setiap program menutup biaya pelaksanaannya dan membiayai kapasitas koordinasi bersama. Pada asumsi yang diungkapkan, penambahan program memperluas layanan setelah kebutuhan kas serta petugas yang menyertainya tersedia (Lampiran X).

English equivalent:

> Each program's contribution pays for its fulfillment and shared coordination capacity. Under the disclosed assumptions, additional programs expand the service once the corresponding staff and cash requirements are funded (Appendix X).

Why it works: it explains the meaning of a financial result. An actual submission must replace this construction with the verified result and relevant sensitivity; it cannot use an appendix label as a substitute for calculation.

### D. Keep accountability in a connected explanation

Weak Indonesian:

> Pemilik menyerahkan; penyedia membawa; penerima memeriksa; koordinator mencatat; institusi membayar.

Stronger construction:

> Pemilik menyerahkan barang kepada pembawa yang telah ditugaskan, kemudian penerima mengakui kedatangannya. Jika pemeriksaan mengubah pekerjaan yang disepakati, koordinator mempertahankan kasus terbuka sampai pihak berwenang memutuskan tindak lanjut dan sumber biayanya.

English equivalent:

> The owner hands the item to the assigned carrier, and the receiving operator acknowledges its arrival. If inspection changes the agreed work, the coordinator keeps the case open until the authorized party determines the next action and its funding.

Why it works: the reader follows a unit through the system and understands why recording a handoff matters. Adapt the work and responsibility chain to the present proposal.

### E. Advance the paragraph instead of announcing itself

Weak ending:

> Hal ini menunjukkan bahwa kolaborasi sangat penting. Penjelasan lebih lengkap dapat dilihat pada Lampiran X.

Stronger construction:

> Kesepakatan tersebut memberi setiap pelaksana pekerjaan yang jelas untuk dipenuhi, sementara pembeli memperoleh satu tempat untuk meninjau kewajiban program (Lampiran X).

English equivalent:

> The agreement gives each operator defined work to fulfill while the buyer has one place to review the program's obligations (Appendix X).

Why it works: it states the consequence. It does not tell the reader that the preceding paragraph was important.

### F. Connect a pilot to the intended scale

Weak:

> A pilot will validate the idea. Further research is needed before national implementation.

Stronger construction:

> The first operating area establishes the contracts, trained staff and records required for a complete program. Expansion repeats those arrangements with local operators and receiving capacity, so each new area brings the resources needed to fulfill its own commitments.

Indonesian equivalent:

> Wilayah pertama menyiapkan kontrak, petugas terlatih, dan catatan yang dibutuhkan untuk menjalankan satu program secara utuh. Perluasan menerapkan tata kerja tersebut bersama pelaksana serta kapasitas penerimaan setempat, sehingga setiap wilayah baru membawa sumber daya untuk memenuhi kewajibannya.

Why it works: it describes a route beyond a test. It does not assert that commissioning has occurred or that every region will accept the arrangement.

## 7. Final editorial pass

1. Identify each paragraph's job in a few words.
2. Remove repeated context, definitions, caveats and thesis summaries.
3. Replace vague labels with actors/actions or remove them.
4. Check every number and citation against the actual source/calculation.
5. Preserve the distinction between proposed, projected and achieved.
6. Read continuously for transitions, concept load and uniform sentence patterns.
7. Place each appendix link at the claim it supports.
8. Check the conclusion adds synthesis rather than new content.
9. Scan explicit author bans across body, captions, tables, links and metadata.
10. Keep assistant commentary, production notes and critic exchanges outside the audience file.

Automated lint can find suspicious strings or repeated patterns. It cannot certify that prose sounds human, matches the author or makes a persuasive argument. Record it as a mechanical aid and perform the editorial judgment.
