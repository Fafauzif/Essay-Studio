# Optional business and financial module

Use this module when the competition asks for a business plan or when the essay's recommendation materially depends on a payer, commercial/institutional purchase, operating costs, capital or financial sustainability. Do not force business frameworks onto an essay that does not need them.

## Contents

1. Connect the argument to the purchase/resource model
2. Define the service unit and market boundary
3. Build a reconstructable financial case
4. Align staffing, capacity and rollout
5. Sensitivity and Monte Carlo
6. Explain the result in the essay
7. Inspection and presentation

## 1. Connect the argument to the purchase/resource model

Identify who pays or supplies scarce resources, what work that contribution purchases, why the arrangement is useful to them and how obligations are reconciled. A beneficiary, owner, operator and payer can be different actors. A program funded by an institution or public/community budget is not automatically a consumer marketplace.

Explain recurring value. A buyer may purchase coordinated access, scheduling, accepted destinations, accountable work, exception follow-up and consolidated reporting. State the specific advantage over arranging those parts separately. Do not assert willingness to pay from the fact that the buyer has a relevant mission or a large budget.

Preserve legitimate alternatives. A specialized dedicated service can be appropriate for a workload that does not fit the proposed shared arrangement. A credible comparison strengthens a contextual choice. Do not imply that every existing provider is a deficient competitor.

## 2. Define the service unit and market boundary

Define when a unit is earned, billed, accepted and complete. These can differ. For example, a service fee tied to accepted receipt need not certify that downstream treatment or reuse has finished. The revenue definition and impact definition must remain separate where the operating stages differ.

For a market/coverage estimate, record:

- The relevant buyer and eligibility filters.
- Geography, reach and time period.
- Price, quantity/use assumptions and currency year.
- Which population count comes from an external source.
- Which conversion, uptake or coverage values are team assumptions.
- Whether a figure is an upper envelope, reachable segment, planned sales or observed demand.

TAM/SAM/SOM can communicate familiar boundaries but does not turn assumptions into market research. A national institution count is an unfiltered ceiling until purchasing and operational eligibility are considered. A team's reachable-program scenario is not a verified count of ready buyers. Use a schematic-circle caption when circle areas are not calculated proportionally.

## 3. Build a reconstructable financial case

Create one coherent model rather than independent attractive tables. Define:

| Component | Required definition |
|---|---|
| Horizon | Start date, monthly/quarterly periods and strategic horizon distinction. |
| Currency | Nominal or constant-year prices; exchange rates/date if used. |
| Revenue | Price, mix, volume, retainer/subscription, service recognition and payment terms. |
| Direct cost | Fulfillment, transport, receiving, review, exception and category costs where relevant. |
| Shared cost | Management, operations, systems, space, professional support and other readiness costs. |
| Labor | Paid time, founder time, workload and capacity step changes. |
| Setup/investment | Initial equipment, onboarding or acquisition costs; avoid double counting. |
| Working capital | Payment lags, advances, receivables, payables and reserves. |
| Tax/finance/distributions | Included or excluded, with consistent result labels. |
| Terminal value | Method if included; otherwise do not add a convenient terminal gain. |
| Funding | Required, planned and committed amounts as separate facts. |

Keep the identities clear:

- **Revenue** records earned service value under the defined rule.
- **Cash receipts** record when that value is collected.
- **Contribution** is the chosen revenue minus relevant direct cost.
- **Operating surplus** must state which shared costs are deducted.
- **Project cash flow** includes the stated investment and operating cash convention.
- **Funding deposits** are financing, not service revenue or a source of project NPV.

Value founder/manager work explicitly where it matters. A positive margin achieved by omitting necessary labor is misleading. Include realistic readiness capacity before first revenue if the implementation plan needs it. Staff blocks, shared overhead and onboarding may create nonlinear thresholds.

Distinguish recurring steady-state break-even from initial commissioning/onboarding cost, a first positive operating month, a sustained sequence of positive months and full project cash payback. Define payback consistently, including whether later deficits can reverse an earlier crossing.

For a discount calculation, align timing and rate. A real annual rate used with constant-year cash should be converted consistently for monthly timing, for example through the annual factor raised to t/12. Do not silently mix nominal inflation with a real discount rate. State whether results are before or after tax, interest and owner distributions.

## 4. Align staffing, capacity and rollout

Tie program/customer additions to actual periods, not merely closing-year counts. A year ending with several active programs does not earn a full year of revenue from all of them. Count contracted months or equivalent units using the rollout schedule.

Reconcile the model with the Gantt and RACI. The first paid service should follow its required contracts, staff, training, records, transport and receiving capability. Adding buyers can require more paid coordination or fulfillment capacity. Do not claim that an interface can absorb unlimited work at no cost.

Account for paid readiness as a resource. Funded hours can exceed calculated workload while allowing preparation, supervision or headroom; explain the definition. Do not rename those hours as jobs created without a valid employment definition. Do not add transport and receiving expenditures to profit after already including them in cost.

## 5. Sensitivity and Monte Carlo

Start with a useful base case and credible downside/upside cases. Use a one-factor sensitivity when it identifies a decisive driver. Add Monte Carlo only when joint uncertainty changes a real decision such as reserve need, capital envelope or operating threshold.

Document the method:

1. Input distributions/ranges and their rationale.
2. Which inputs are independent and which are derived/dependent.
3. Category mix and any capacity/staffing response.
4. Generator, sample count, seed and exact primitive draw order.
5. Whether one draw applies throughout the horizon or changes each period.
6. Output definition, quantile method and reporting units.
7. Numerical stability check appropriate to the stated decision.
8. Interpretation of output frequency and association.

Do not assign distributions that imply collected market evidence when they are team ranges. Correlated acquisition and workload or linked cost drivers should not be treated as independent upside opportunities merely to make the result attractive. A seed without draw order may not reproduce the published realization.

State the meaning of P10/P50/P90. A conditional positive-NPV frequency is a frequency within specified planning distributions; it is not a measured probability of market success. Rank association can identify influential inputs without establishing causal effects.

If a planned funding amount covers only a small part of simulated reserve needs, show that fact and propose a credible staged capital decision if justified. Distinguish committed capital from a recommended envelope. Do not hide adverse cases or describe a contingent amount as already secured.

## 6. Explain the result in the essay

Keep the financial conclusion, its operating meaning and a decisive sensitivity in the main text. Define an unfamiliar acronym such as nilai kini bersih (NPV) on first use when the audience needs it. Move the full category costs, monthly schedules, equations and distributions to linked appendices.

A useful paragraph answers:

- What does a buyer pay and what work does it fund?
- What scale covers the ongoing work under stated assumptions?
- What does growth require in staff and cash?
- What funding is proposed, and what drives the need for headroom?
- Why does the result support the bounded implementation choice?

Avoid a sequence of many exact capital figures when two decisive results and an explanation are enough. Do not use a standalone appendix pointer as the paragraph's conclusion. Do not repeatedly apologize for using a model; label its scope where interpretation requires it and discuss the intended operating judgment directly.

## 7. Inspection and presentation

Reconstruct the critical calculations independently when they are consequential. Check weighted category averages, time-unit counts, staffing workload, annual aggregates, cash timing, funding/receivable separation, break-even, NPV and payback. Verify that totals come from unrounded data and display rounding is consistent.

Use native tables for exact schedules and assumptions. Use the chart tools for annual comparisons, cash curves, sensitivity, distributions and drivers. Reconcile chart series with the actual model output. State units in titles/axes/headers. Keep numeric alignment consistent and avoid fused labels or narrow headers.

The JejakBalik finance source is an archived worked case. Its buyer numbers, prices, staff costs, seed, dates and institutional scope are not default assumptions for a new project. Reuse its calculation disciplines and inspectable structure, not its favorable numerical result.
