# Appendices and navigation

Use this reference to design a detailed dossier, build internal links, compress supporting material or repair appendix readability.

## 1. Two-layer argument

The essay explains and judges the proposal. Appendices make the supporting work inspectable. A judge should understand the intended operation, distinctive mechanism, principal rationale, implementation route and bounded contribution with appendices closed.

Do not move the central causal bridge into an appendix merely to meet a page limit. Do not turn an unrestricted appendix allowance into a second repetitive essay. Detailed work can be extensive while its presentation remains economical: use exact tables, charts, diagrams, calculations, sources and useful captures.

Keep main-body headline results and practical interpretation. Move derivations, full schedules, category detail, source scope, complete matrices and method reconstruction behind them. A paragraph ending cannot routinely consist of an advertisement for what is in an appendix.

Appendix permission and any exemptions must come from the actual competition. Do not assume an unlimited appendix simply because JejakBalik permitted one.

## 2. Exhibit admission and contract

Admit an exhibit when it answers a reader/judge question that matters to the case. Record internally:

| Field | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Reader question | What can the reader determine here? |
| Supported claim | Which precise body claim or implementation decision uses it? |
| Evidence/input | Source, observation, planning assumption or calculation. |
| Method | Transformation, comparison, classification or equation. |
| Format | Native table, precise chart/diagram, actual screenshot or conceptual image. |
| Physical requirement | Width/height, readable type, section placement and orientation. |
| Interpretation | Result and decisive scope/assumption note. |
| Navigation | Stable ID, index entry, incoming claim links and return target. |

Remove duplicated figures or prose that do not add another decision. Several framework tables may share one action table rather than repeating action interpretations in every matrix. A complex equation can have a compact definition table instead of several pages of explanatory throat-clearing.

Choose stable IDs independently from page numbers. Reordering sections must update headings, figure captions, body links, index entries, internal cross-references and returns. Record a migration map for old feedback. Consolidated subsections do not necessarily follow a one-to-one letter shift.

## 3. Native tables and analytical figures

Use native DOCX tables first for editable comparisons, assumptions, responsibilities, scores, risk registers, SMART definitions and schedules that fit as tables. Use TikZ/PGFPlots for precise branching, flows, nested diagrams, Gantt charts, numerical relationships and distributions when they communicate more clearly.

Avoid flattening an ordinary table into an image to bypass formatting work. Avoid building an exact information chart through generative imagery. Preserve the original data/source and chart source so a future revision does not require guessing values from pixels.

Use deliberate widths. Give explanatory columns room and keep numeric/code columns proportionate. The author's house style favors justified long narrative cells, readable short labels and consistently aligned numbers. Left-align short labels where justification creates gaps; center compact categories and right-align comparable numeric amounts. Do not split words merely to preserve equal column widths.

Use restrained headers and white body rows. No automatic alternating fills, checkerboards, heavy ornamental borders, card-grid decoration or slide-style banners. Competition rules may require monochrome or another template. The muted sage palette is a preference, not a requirement overriding the guidebook.

Repeat table headers across legitimate continuations. Prevent an individual data row from splitting when practical. Keep the final source/assumption note with at least a meaningful last data row. Do not keep an entire long table together and create large empty areas.

The original user explicitly accepted 10 pt appendix tables in JejakBalik; a new project must check its own rules and author permission. Ordinary prose remains at the guidebook's required size. Never shrink essential figures or tables below readable physical size to make them fit.

## 4. Claim-level links and returns

Attach a real internal link to the sentence or claim it supports. Use a visible restrained treatment, such as bold underlined black text, where the template permits it. A blue default hyperlink is not necessary. A visually ordinary word that secretly links is insufficient when the author expects discoverable navigation.

Constructed placement examples:

> Pada asumsi tim, kapasitas yang dibiayai mencukupi kebutuhan kerja program (Lampiran H.2). Perluasan mengikuti jadwal penambahan petugas yang menyertai kontrak baru.

> Under the disclosed assumptions, funded staff capacity covers the program workload (Appendix H.2). Expansion follows the staffing schedule attached to new contracts.

These examples demonstrate link position; their placeholder IDs are not final exhibit assignments.

Avoid listing six appendices at the end of a paragraph whose individual claims cannot be matched to them. A sentence containing two related claims may need two specific links. Do not add a redundant link after every ordinary sentence when no separate support is needed; the goal is precise claim-to-evidence mapping.

Make the appendix index link each specific subsection/exhibit by its ID and meaningful title. Broad group letters alone are insufficient for a long dossier. Each section, including an access/QR section created outside a general rendering loop, needs explicit visible return navigation to the relevant essay section and/or index.

Choose relevant return targets: financial details return to the financial argument, implementation details to implementation, impact definitions to the contribution discussion. A link that resolves to an existing bookmark can still be semantically wrong. Check actual destination content, not only target existence.

Do not add audience prose explaining how the hyperlinks work. The visible labels and index should make navigation self-evident.

## 5. Continuous pagination

Let appendices flow continuously. Use a new page for a new major part when its structure justifies it, not automatically for each appendix. The reference tail, access section and index may share a page when their hierarchy remains clear and the rules allow it.

Use keep-with-next chains selectively:

1. Appendix heading.
2. Short return navigation.
3. First substantive table title, figure/image or table beginning.

Keep an image with its caption. Keep essential notes with their data. Do not chain the whole section, every table row and the following section together; this can push large blocks forward and create artificial gaps.

Plan image groups around reading jobs. For example, a network overview may share a page with two short exception-state captures, while a tall options panel pairs with a related approval view in a native two-column table. Display form details through faithful crops instead of a tiny modal floating inside a large darkened screen. Preserve values, status, controls and provenance.

Distinguish purposeful breathing room from accidental emptiness. A last page can end naturally. A header-only note continuation, a half-empty page caused by one oversized screenshot, or an isolated heading before a figure is a repairable production defect. Rearrange related material, change proportional column widths or choose a faithful crop before reducing required font sizes.

Render after material reflow changes. An apparently compact source specification does not prove a compact actual document.

## 6. Source notes, captions and assumptions

Use captions to identify the content and its meaning. State units, category definitions, legend and necessary scope close to the exhibit. Keep captions concise; do not use them to narrate the assistant's workflow or claim that a chart is “presentation-ready.”

Use one compact source/assumption note where it is sufficient. A detached chart may need its own local boundary even if the body already states it. Repetition is justified by interpretation, not by a fixed caveat quota.

Use “Estimasi tim” / “Team assumptions” for adopted planning inputs and “Ilustrasi” / “Illustration” for constructed examples. Do not label estimates as AI-generated values or attribute them to a source that supplied only context. Cite external evidence through meaningful authors/titles, not upload filenames, tool IDs or internal cell coordinates.

For schematic nested circles, say they are schematic when readers might otherwise infer area proportionality. For a risk heatmap, identify whether initial, residual or both positions are plotted. For an outcome graph, make the reporting stage and denominator visible. Numerical accuracy includes the caption's interpretation.

## 7. Prototype and access exhibits

Include prototype access when it contributes to the submission and is allowed. A QR-first appendix was an explicit JejakBalik choice; do not make every essay start its dossier with a QR.

If a QR is included:

- Encode the owner-approved intended URL.
- Print a readable address or meaningful access label.
- Decode the actual embedded QR and compare its payload.
- Inspect actual access and describe the scope of that check.
- Verify whether the public release matches the illustrated local version.
- Use a faithful label if an older interface is accessible; do not claim a current deployment.

A source-code link may accompany the demo. It must not silently replace the user's intended demo destination. If deployment is not authorized, record the release gap and continue the essay task; do not publish or rebuild the app to make a QR claim convenient.

Separate interface evidence from conceptual illustrations and operating evidence. A local screenshot may demonstrate a calculation/status behavior. A user-supplied 3D scene may visualize intended work. Neither is a photograph of a commissioned facility or proof that real participants completed the process.

## 8. Final inspection

Check every final page at its actual placed size. Verify the index, each appendix start, all table continuations, captions, screenshot detail, chart labels and end pages. Check source bytes or embedded image hashes when a claimed visual fix could still be using an old image.

Test internal link existence and meaningful destination. Check exported PDF navigation as well as DOCX bookmarks. Scan for unused or duplicate IDs, references to removed subsections and old figure numbering. Test the exact QR/image embedded in the delivery file.

Record actual page counts and source/file identities in the handover. A later source change or different export needs an affected-area recheck; a previous screenshot of a good page is not proof that the current file retains it.
